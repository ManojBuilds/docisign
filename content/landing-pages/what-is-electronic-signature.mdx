## Understanding Electronic Signatures in 2026

An electronic signature, or e-signature, is a digital representation of a person's intent to agree to the content of a document or a set of data. While it might look like a simple image of your handwriting, a modern e-signature is a sophisticated piece of digital evidence.

In the United States, the **ESIGN Act** and **UETA** established that electronic signatures are legally valid and cannot be denied legal effect solely because they are electronic.

## Defining the Three Levels of E-Signatures

Not all electronic signatures are created equal. Depending on the jurisdiction (especially under eIDAS in the EU), there are three main types:

### 1. Simple Electronic Signatures (SES)
This is the most common type. It includes typing your name, ticking a box, or drawing your signature on a screen. Boopsign's "No Account" approach uses SES with added security layers to make it both user-friendly and robust.

### 2. Advanced Electronic Signatures (AES)
These require a higher level of signer identification and are uniquely linked to the signer. They are typically based on digital certificates.

### 3. Qualified Electronic Signatures (QES)
The most secure level, requiring a face-to-face (or equivalent) ID check and a qualified signature creation device. These are usually reserved for high-stakes governmental or financial transactions.

## Electronic vs. Digital Signatures: What's the Difference?

These terms are often used interchangeably, but they have distinct technical meanings:

- **Electronic Signature:** The broad category of all ways to sign digitally. It focuses on the *intent* of the signer.
- **Digital Signature:** A specific technology (using Public Key Infrastructure or PKI) that *implements* an electronic signature. Think of it as the secure "envelope" that ensures the document hasn't been tampered with.

**Boopsign combines both:** You get the ease of an electronic signature with the underlying security technology of a digital signature.
