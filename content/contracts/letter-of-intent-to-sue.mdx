The first step in legal resolution. This formal letter documents your grievance, states your demands, and provides a final opportunity for settlement before filing a lawsuit.

## What's inside this letter of intent?

A professionally structured demand letter designed to be taken seriously by individuals, businesses, and legal departments.

### Key Features

- **Statement of Facts Section**: A clear, chronological account of the events leading to the dispute.
- **Specific Demands Clause**: Clearly outlines exactly what is required to satisfy the claim and avoid a lawsuit.
- **Reservation of Rights**: Standard legal language that ensures you are not giving up any other legal options by sending this letter.
- **Settlement Deadline**: Sets a firm date for a response, showing the recipient that you are serious about moving to the next stage.

## Why documentation matters

Courts look favorably on parties who attempt to resolve disputes without litigation. This letter serves as your primary evidence of that attempt.

- **Professional Tone**: Signals that you are prepared for legal action.
- **Evidence Building**: Documents your side of the story for the record.
- **Cost Effective**: Often resolves disputes for the cost of a stamp.

## Common Questions

### Do I have to send a Letter of Intent to Sue before filing a lawsuit?

In many jurisdictions and for certain types of cases (like small claims or medical malpractice), sending a formal demand letter is a legal requirement. Even when not required, it is highly recommended as it shows the court you made a good-faith effort to resolve the dispute.

### What should I include in the letter?

You should include a clear statement of the facts, the legal basis for your claim, the specific amount of money or action required to settle, a firm deadline for a response, and a statement that you will pursue all legal remedies if the deadline is missed.

### How should I deliver this letter?

It is best to send this letter via Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested. This provides legal proof that the recipient received the letter, which can be critical evidence if the case goes to court.

### Will this letter guarantee a settlement?

No, but it often prompts the other party to take your claim seriously and engage in settlement discussions to avoid the time and expense of a lawsuit.
