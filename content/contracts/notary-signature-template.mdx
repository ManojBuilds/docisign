Ensure your notarized documents are legally valid. This template provides the standard blocks for signatures, notary seals, and state-specific acknowledgment language.

## What's inside this notary template?

Essential components for any document requiring professional or legal notarization.

### Key Features

- **Standard Acknowledgment Block**: The most common form for verifying that a signature was made voluntarily.
- **Verification Upon Oath (Jurat)**: Specifically for documents like affidavits where the signer is swearing to the truth of the contents.
- **Venue Section (State/County)**: Mandatory section for the notary to specify the exact location where the notarization occurred.
- **Notary Seal Space**: Properly formatted area for the notary to place their physical or digital stamp without obscuring text.

## Why formatting matters for notaries

Notaries often cannot provide legal forms themselves. Having the correct signature block already on your document saves time and prevents rejection by the notary or receiving party.

- **Universal Design**: Works for deeds, wills, and affidavits.
- **Compliance**: Includes all required data fields for US notaries.
- **Clarity**: Clearly identifies who is signing and when.

## Common Questions

### What is the difference between an Acknowledgment and a Jurat?

An Acknowledgment is used to verify the identity of the signer and that they signed the document voluntarily. A Jurat is used when the signer must take an oath or affirmation that the contents of the document are true (common for affidavits).

### Can I use an electronic signature for notarization?

Yes, many states now allow Remote Online Notarization (RON). However, the notary must still witness the signing through a secure audio-video link and use a digital seal. This template can be used for both physical and electronic notarization.

### What happens if a notary block is incorrect?

If the notary block doesn't meet state requirements, the notarization may be rejected by government offices or courts. Using a standard template like this helps ensure you have the necessary elements (State, County, Date, Signer Name).

### Do notaries need to read the whole document?

No. A notary's job is to verify the identity of the signer and ensure they are signing willingly. They are not verifying the legal accuracy or content of the document itself.
