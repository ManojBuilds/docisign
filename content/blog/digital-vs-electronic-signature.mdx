"Electronic signature" and "Digital signature" sound like synonyms, but in the world of cybersecurity and law, they refer to two very different things. 

Think of it like this: **Electronic Signature** is the broad category (like "Vehicle"), while **Digital Signature** is a specific, high-security method within that category (like a "Tesla with Biometric Locks").

In this deep dive, we’ll look at the technical mechanisms that define both and help you decide which level of security your business requires.

## What is an Electronic Signature?
An electronic signature (e-signature) is a legal concept. It is defined by the intent of the signer to agree to the contents of a document. It can be:
-   A scanned image of a handwritten signature.
-   A typed name at the end of an email.
-   Clicking an "I Agree" button.
-   Drawing your name on a touchscreen.

**The Focus:** Legality and intent. As long as you can prove the person meant to sign, it is generally considered an e-signature.

## What is a Digital Signature?
A digital signature is a technical implementation. It uses a specific type of encryption called **Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)** to bind a digital certificate to the document. 

A digital signature provides three things that a basic e-signature cannot:
1.  **Authentication:** It proves exactly who signed the document using a third-party certificate.
2.  **Integrity:** it proves the document has not been altered since the moment it was signed.
3.  **Non-Repudiation:** It prevents the signer from later claiming they didn't sign it, due to the unique cryptographic keys used.

## The Technical Mechanism: How Digital Signatures Work
When you sign a document with a digital signature (like the ones generated by Boopsign):

1.  **Hashing:** A mathematical "hash" (a unique string of characters) is created from your document. If a single comma is changed in the document later, the hash will change completely.
2.  **Encryption:** This hash is encrypted using your "Private Key."
3.  **The Signature:** The encrypted hash is attached to the document.
4.  **Verification:** When someone opens the document, the system uses your "Public Key" to decrypt the hash. If the decrypted hash matches the document’s current hash, the signature is valid.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | Electronic Signature (Standard) | Digital Signature (Secure) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Verification** | Based on Email/IP/ID | Based on Cryptographic Keys |
| **Tamper-Proof** | Traces changes in audit log | Breaks the digital seal immediately |
| **Complexity** | Very Low | High (under the hood) |
| **Regulatory Standing** | ESIGN / UETA | eIDAS (High) / HIPAA / GSA |
