Getting a new contract is exciting, but that excitement can lead to "Fine Print Blindness." Many freelancers sign agreements without realizing they’ve just committed to unlimited work, waived their IP rights, or agreed to get paid "whenever the client feels like it."

In this guide, we'll break down the 10 most common red flags in freelance contracts and show you how to swap them for terms that protect your time and your income.

## 1. Vague "Scope of Work"
**The Red Flag:** Terms like "all necessary services" or "any work required to complete the project."

This is the birth of **Scope Creep**. If your contract doesn't define the edges of the project, you’re essentially giving the client a blank check on your time.

<Callout variant="info">
  **The Fix:** Use specific numbers. Instead of "Brand Design," use "3 logo concepts, 2 rounds of revisions, and 1 style guide."
</Callout>

## 2. "Unlimited" Revisions
**The Red Flag:** No cap on revision rounds or language like "until client satisfaction."

Revisions are part of the creative process, but "unlimited" is a business killer. It turns a $2,000 project into a $5/hour job if the client is indecisive.

**Safe Alternative:** "Flat fee includes two (2) rounds of revisions. Subsequent rounds billed at $150/hour."

## 3. "Upon Client Satisfaction" Payment
**The Red Flag:** Tying your final payment to the client’s subjective "satisfaction."

You can meet every technical requirement, but if the client "isn't feeling it," this clause gives them a legal loophole to withhold payment indefinitely.

## 4. IP Transfer BEFORE Payment
**The Red Flag:** "The Designer hereby assigns all rights to the Client upon delivery."

If you transfer the Intellectual Property (IP) as soon as you send the files, you lose your biggest piece of leverage for getting paid.

**The Fix:** Ensure the contract states that IP transfer only happens **after full payment is received.**

## 5. Indefinite Termination Clauses
**The Red Flag:** Client can terminate "for any reason" without notice or compensation for work-in-progress.

You shouldn't be penalized if a client’s budget gets cut. You deserve to be paid for the hours you've invested up to the point of termination.

## 6. Blind Indemnification
**The Red Flag:** You agree to pay for the client's legal fees if they get sued for anything related to your work.

These clauses can be incredibly dangerous. If you use a font that the client mistakenly thinks they own, you could be on the hook for their $50,000 legal bill.

<Callout variant="warning">
  **Legal Warning:** Always try to strike "indemnification" clauses entirely, or at least limit them to things you had *direct* control over (like your own gross negligence).
</Callout>

## 7. Non-Compete Clauses (The "Career Killer")
**The Red Flag:** You are forbidden from working for anyone in the same industry for 2 years.

For a specialized freelancer (e.g., a Fintech copywriter), this effectively bans you from working with 90% of your potential client base.

## 8. "Work for Hire" vs. Licensing
**The Red Flag:** Blanket use of "Work for Hire" language.

In many jurisdictions, "Work for Hire" means the client owns everything, including your discarded concepts and early sketches. Consider **licensing** the final work instead while retaining ownership of your "background technology" or "process."

## 9. No Late Fees or Interest
**The Red Flag:** A contract that doesn't mention penalties for late payment.

Without a late fee, your invoice is the client's last priority. Interest and fees give them a financial incentive to stay on schedule.

## 10. Manual Post-Signing Processes
**The Red Flag:** Requesting a "wet signature" or a scanned copy via email.

This isn't a legal red flag, but it's a **process red flag**. If a client makes it hard to start the relationship, they will likely make it hard to finish the project.
