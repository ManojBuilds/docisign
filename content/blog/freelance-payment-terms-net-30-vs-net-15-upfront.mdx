As a freelancer, you’re not just the talent—you’re the CFO. One of the most important financial decisions you’ll make isn't how much you charge, but **when you get paid.** 

Inconsistent payment terms are the #1 cause of "Freelancer Burnout." When you’re waiting 45 days for a check while your rent is due on the 1st, you’re not running a business—you’re giving out interest-free loans.

Let's break down the three standard payment structures and when to use each.

---

## 1. Upfront Payments (The Gold Standard)
**Best for:** New clients, small projects, and fixed-fee engagements.

Requesting a deposit (usually 50% upfront) is the industry standard for independent professionals. It ensures that the client is "skin in the game" and covers your overhead while you work.

<Callout variant="success">
  **The "Commitment" Factor:** A client who refuses to pay any amount upfront is often a client who will be difficult to collect from later. Treat the deposit as a filter for high-quality clients.
</Callout>

## 2. Net 15 (The Professional Middle Ground)
**Best for:** Ongoing retainers and medium-sized agencies.

"Net 15" means the client has 15 calendar days to pay the invoice from the date it was issued. In the world of modern e-payments, 15 days is more than enough time for any professional company to process an invoice. 

**Pro Tip:** If you use Net 15, set up automated reminders in your invoicing tool for Day 7 (friendly nudge) and Day 16 (past due notice).

## 3. Net 30 (The Corporate Standard)
**Best for:** Large enterprises and Fortune 500 companies.

Most large corporations have complex accounting cycles and won't agree to anything faster than Net 30. While it can be annoying, working with these "whales" often comes with higher project fees that make the wait worth it.

---

## Side-by-Side: Which Terms When?

| Client Type | Recommended Terms | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Boutique Startup** | 100% Upfront (Small) / 50% (Large) | Cash flow is king; high risk of "pivoting." |
| **Mid-Market Agency** | Net 15 | They have a billing dept but move fast. |
| **Enterprise Corp** | Net 30 | Their systems are slow; they are reliable but rigid. |
| **"Problem" Client** | 100% Upfront | If they've paid late before, remove the risk. |

---

## How to Negotiate Better Terms
Many freelancers are afraid to ask for better terms, fearing they'll lose the gig. But professional clients respect professional boundaries.

**The Script:**
> *"For all new collaborations, my standard policy is a 50% commencement deposit. This allows me to reserve your spot on my calendar and begin work immediately. The remaining 50% is billed on Net 15 terms upon final delivery. Does that work for your billing cycle?"*
