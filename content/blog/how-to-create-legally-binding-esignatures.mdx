Electronic signatures are legally valid in most countries, but the signature process has to be trustworthy. This guide shows you how to create legally binding eSignatures with the right steps and documentation.

## What Makes an eSignature Legally Binding?

A legally binding eSignature generally requires:

- Clear intent to sign
- Agreement to do business electronically
- A way to verify who signed
- A tamper-evident record of the final document

## Step 1: Use a Signing Method That Records Intent

A checkbox, “I agree” button, or signature field helps capture intent. The signer should be guided through a clear signing flow so it’s obvious they are agreeing to the document.

## Step 2: Verify the Signer’s Identity

The simplest approach is email verification. More sensitive documents may require additional checks like one-time codes or identity verification.

## Step 3: Capture an Audit Trail

A proper audit trail should include:

- Timestamps for each step
- IP address and device details
- The exact document version that was signed

This is the evidence you’ll need if the signature is ever disputed.

## Step 4: Use a Tamper-Evident Document

Once signed, the document should be sealed so changes are detectable. Most eSignature platforms generate a final PDF with a certificate of completion.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Sending a raw PDF without audit evidence
- Allowing edits after signing
- Using tools that do not track signer activity

## When to Get Legal Advice

This guide is informational only. If you’re dealing with complex contracts or regulated industries, consult a qualified attorney in your jurisdiction.
