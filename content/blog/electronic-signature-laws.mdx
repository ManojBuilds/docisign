The transition from "wet-ink" signatures to digital ones has been supported by robust legal frameworks for over two decades. However, many business owners and freelancers still worry: *Is this electronic signature actually legal?*

The short answer is: **Yes.** In almost every major economy, electronic signatures carry the same legal weight as traditional paper-based signatures. In this guide, we'll break down the key laws (ESIGN, UETA, eIDAS) and help you understand when and how to use them.

## The Core Principles of E-Signature Legality

For an electronic signature to be legally binding, most laws require four key elements:

1.  **Intent to Sign:** The signer must demonstrate a clear intent to sign the document (e.g., clicking a "Sign" button or typing their name).
2.  **Consent to Do Business Electronically:** Parties must agree to conduct the transaction electronically.
3.  **Association of Signature with the Record:** The system must capture the process by which the signature was created and associate it with the specific document.
4.  **Record Retention:** The signed document must be stored in a way that allows it to be retrieved and viewed by all parties later.

## Key Electronic Signature Laws

### 1. United States: ESIGN Act & UETA
In the U.S., electronic signatures are governed at both the federal and state levels.

*   **The ESIGN Act (2000):** A federal law that ensures a contract or signature "may not be denied legal effect, validity, or enforceability solely because it is in electronic form."
*   **UETA (Uniform Electronic Transactions Act):** Adopted by 49 states, UETA provides a similar framework to ESIGN, ensuring that electronic records and signatures are legally valid.

### 2. European Union: eIDAS
The **eIDAS Regulation (2016)** provides a uniform legal framework for electronic identification and trust services across all EU member states. It defines three levels of electronic signatures:

-   **Simple Electronic Signature (SES):** The most common (and what Boopsign typically uses). It's legally binding for most business transactions.
-   **Advanced Electronic Signature (AES):** Requires higher levels of identity verification.
-   **Qualified Electronic Signature (QES):** The highest level, involving a face-to-face (or equivalent) verification process.

## When E-Signatures Are (and Aren't) Valid

While electronic signatures are incredibly versatile, there are a few specific legal documents that still require a physical, wet-ink signature in many jurisdictions.
