Microsoft Word is the world's most popular tool for drafting contracts, but when it comes to *signing* them, most people are doing it wrong.

Whether you're a freelancer closing a new client or a consultant finalizing a proposal, the way you sign your document matters. While you can technically insert a picture of your signature or use Word's built-in signature line, these methods are often insecure, unprofessional, and potentially unenforceable in a court of law.

In this guide, we’ll dive into why raw Word documents are a liability and show you the fastest, most secure way to get your agreements signed and sealed.

## The Hidden Risks of Signing Directly in Word

If you simply type your name or paste a JPEG of your signature into a `.docx` file, you're leaving your business vulnerable. Here's why:

1.  **The "Post-Signature Edit" Risk**: A Word document is an open file. Even after you sign it, anyone with a basic copy of Word can open the file, delete your signature, change a "Net 30" payment term to "Net 90," and paste your signature back in. 
2.  **Zero Evidence Trail**: A legal signature requires proof of intent and attribution. Standard Word files don't track who signed, when they signed, or the IP address used. If a client denies signing, you have zero metadata to prove otherwise.
3.  **Signer Friction**: Sending a raw `.docx` file forces your client to download the file, open it in Word (which they might not have on their phone), sign it, save it, and email it back. Every extra step increases the chance they'll "do it later"—and eventually forget.

Once the signing process is finished, Boopsign generates a **Certificate of Completion** for every document. This is your "Legal Insurance Policy." It contains:
