Microsoft Word is the world's most popular tool for drafting contracts, but when it comes to *signing* them, it can be a bit tricky. 

While you can technically insert a picture of your signature or use Word's built-in signature line, these methods often lack the security and "tamper-evidence" required for a truly legal agreement. In this guide, we’ll show you the most secure and professional way to sign a Word document.

## The Problem with Signing Directly in Word

1.  **Easily Modified:** A signature in a Word document is just an image or a text field. Anyone with Word can open the file, delete your signature, or change the terms of the contract *after* you’ve signed it.
2.  **No Audit Trail:** Standard Word documents don't track who signed, when they signed, or what their IP address was. If a dispute arises, you have very little evidence.
3.  **Friction for the Signer:** Sending a `.docx` file forces your client to have Word installed and opens the door for them to make un-tracked changes (redlines) without your knowledge.

## The Solution: Convert and Sign

The industry standard for signing Word documents is to convert them to PDF first. This "freezes" the layout and content. Boopsign makes this entire process seamless.

### Step 1: Upload Your Word File
You don't even need to save your Word document as a PDF yourself. Just drag and drop your `.docx` file directly into [Boopsign](/) or our [Online Signature Maker](/tools/online-signature-maker). Our platform will automatically and securely convert it for you.

### Step 2: Add Recipients
Enter the names and email addresses of everyone who needs to sign. You can even set a signing order if you need your client to sign before you counter-sign.

### Step 3: Place Your Signature Fields
Open our editor and drag the "Signature," "Date," and "Text" fields onto the document. Since the file is now a PDF, these fields are anchored perfectly to the page.

### Step 4: Send and Sign
Click "Send." Your client will receive a secure link. They can sign from their phone or desktop in seconds. Once complete, everyone gets a "Sealed" PDF that cannot be modified without breaking the digital signature.
