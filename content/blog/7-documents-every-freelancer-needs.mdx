Many freelancers start their careers with a "gentleman’s agreement" and a handshake. But as your business grows, you'll quickly realize that a handshake doesn't stand up in court, and an email thread isn't a substitute for a legal contract.

Protecting yourself with the right paperwork isn't about being "difficult"—it's about being professional. Here are the 7 essential documents every freelancer should have in their legal toolkit.

## 1. The Freelance Service Agreement
This is your "Master Contract." It defines the overarching relationship between you and your client. It should cover the big-picture items like payment terms, intellectual property, confidentiality, and how to end the relationship.

<Callout variant="info">
  **Pro Tip:** Once you have a signed Service Agreement, you don't need to re-negotiate these terms for every new project with that client. You simply refer back to it.
</Callout>

## 2. Statement of Work (SOW)
While the Service Agreement defines the *relationship*, the SOW defines the *project*. This is a highly detailed document that outlines:
-   **Specific Deliverables:** Exactly what the client is getting.
-   **Milestones:** When each part of the work is due.
-   **Success Metrics:** How both parties know the work is "done."

## 3. Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)
Clients often share sensitive business data, trade secrets, or upcoming product launches. An NDA ensures that you won't share their secrets, and—importantly—that they won't share *your* proprietary processes or rates with your competitors.

## 4. Intellectual Property (IP) Assignment
Who owns the work once the invoice is paid? This document clarifies whether you are selling the client **ownership** of the work (common for logos) or a **license** to use the work (common for software or photography).

## 5. Independent Contractor Agreement
This document is vital for tax and employment law compliance. It explicitly states that you are *not* an employee. This protects the client from being audited for missing payroll taxes and protects you from being treated like a subordinate instead of a partner.

## 6. Project Change Order
This is the most underrated document in a freelancer's toolkit. When a client says, "Could you also just add this one little thing?", you send a Change Order. It documents the change in scope and the adjusted price/timeline.

## 7. Termination Notice (The "Kill Fee" Clause)
What happens if the project is cancelled halfway through? A clear termination clause (or "Kill Fee") ensures that if the client pulls the plug, you are still compensated for the hours you've already invested.
